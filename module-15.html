MODULE 15 · PRODUCT ENGINEERING

Build the workflows that make a business run.

Payments are a specialization, but they teach a broader engineering lesson: external systems are asynchronous, failure-prone, security-sensitive, and governed by business rules.

1. Payment architecture

Keep payment details out of your application whenever the provider can host sensitive collection. Your server creates a payment operation, the browser completes the approved interaction, and your server treats verified provider events as authoritative inputs to the business workflow.

Browser → Your server → Payment provider
                    ↓
             verified webhook
                    ↓
              billing state

2. Payment Intents & checkout

Learn the distinction between starting a payment, confirming it, and recording the resulting business state. Stripe's Payment Intents and Checkout APIs provide different integration models; choose based on the required control and user experience.

3. Webhook reliability

Never assume a webhook arrives exactly once or in order. Verify the provider signature, persist an event identifier, make processing idempotent, and return a successful response only after your system has safely accepted the event.

receive → verify signature → deduplicate
        → validate event → apply transition
        → record event → acknowledge
Key rule: a browser redirect is useful for UX, but a verified server-to-server event is the stronger source for payment state.

4. Subscription models

  • Flat subscription: one price for the account.
  • Seat-based: price depends on active users/seats.
  • Usage-based: price follows measured consumption.
  • Tiered: different ranges unlock different prices or capabilities.
  • Metered billing: usage is collected and billed on a defined schedule.

5. Billing state machine

Do not model billing as one boolean called paid. Model meaningful states and transitions: trial, active, past due, paused, canceled, refunded, and any provider-specific states that affect access.

6. Business edge cases

  • Payment fails after a user believes checkout succeeded.
  • A webhook is delivered twice.
  • A webhook arrives after a refund.
  • A subscription changes mid-cycle.
  • A card expires.
  • A customer downgrades before the next billing period.
  • Provider downtime delays state synchronization.

7. Capstone — SaaS billing sandbox

Design a subscription workflow without storing raw card data. Define your local billing state, provider identifiers, webhook event table, idempotency strategy, entitlement rules, cancellation flow and recovery process. Use provider test mode for actual experiments.
